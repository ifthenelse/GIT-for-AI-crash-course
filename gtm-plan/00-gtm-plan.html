All five steps are confirmed; the roadmap and consolidation were approved after the cross-document plan-qa check. This document does not claim completed implementation or authorize publication, outreach, or services.

1. Executive summary

The independent public course is positioned as a visual Git collaboration lab. Confirmed promise, IT: «Collabora con Git sapendo cosa cambia e come recuperare il lavoro.» EN: “Collaborate with Git knowing what changes and how to recover your work.” The path connects changes, commits, review, policies, and recovery through three complete tiers in Italian and English.

Confirmed distribution uses three engines: useful scenarios for discovery, a worked example for choosing/starting the course, and voluntary feedback for correcting the path. Zero-cost services, at most three initial channels, and post-launch maintenance of 1–2 hours/week are constraints, not effectiveness forecasts.

The first 90 days organize six initiatives dependent on capacity and evidence: feasibility, representative case, private review, complete release candidate, authorized release, and first maintenance cycle. The schedule is indicative; if time or evidence is insufficient, release moves. Do not reduce tiers or languages or buy a plan to bypass blockers.

2. Business snapshot

Public project at the analysis stage, from a public repository. Participants: Engineers, Architects, and business professionals with practical technical experience; no stated geographic restriction. Access without a sales process or required payment. No baseline for traffic, starts, or learning is available.

Frontend planned on Pages; React/Vite, TypeScript, Zustand/Zundo, and i18next; free external API for identity, validation, and progress. Social SSO in phases, with GitHub proposed first. Complete Italian and English content is a launch requirement.

Andrea Collet owns the repository; this does not automatically assign operational roles. Initial capacity and owners remain undefined; 1–2 hours/week is confirmed only after launch. Full text: snapshot.

3. Market map

Three layers of alternatives: interactive simulators; real-platform practice and technical references; mentoring or research when a problem occurs. Choice frequencies and market shares are unknown.

  • LearnGitBranching: graph, sandbox, levels, and commands in the browser. Visualizing Git alone does not demonstrate how the course differs.
  • Oh My Git!: game with visualized structures and real Git; advertised desktop path and stated limited maintenance. These do not prove inferior quality.
  • GitHub Skills: exercises in the participant's repository and platform feedback, an alternative based on real practice.
  • Microsoft Learn and Azure Repos: training and documentation on concepts, PRs, and policies, with distinct prerequisites and resources.
  • Pro Git: progressive reference on basics, workflows, and internals; the Italian edition shows uneven translation according to the page consulted.

Qualitative axes: simulation/real practice; mental model/collaboration; exercise/reference. The course's position is designed, not measured. GAP HYPOTHESIS: follow an incident from file to policy; explain recovery through visible consequences. Relevance, an unclaimed position, and feasibility are unproven. Complete map and sources.

4. Pillars

P1 — From file to review

  • Candidate claim: connect a change to its effects on history and shared review.
  • Relevant: plausible for the stated audiences; no direct evidence is available yet.
  • Differentiated: hypothesis against LearnGitBranching's graph emphasis and GitHub Skills' real-work emphasis; no exclusivity demonstrated.
  • Defensible: not yet passed; synchronized views are specified, without observable implementation.
  • Evidence needed: working scenario and a participant who can explain files, index, commits, and PRs. Status: unverified-claim P1.

P2 — The same problem, two contexts

  • Candidate claim: recognize common Git principles and operational differences between GitHub and Azure DevOps.
  • Relevant: consistent with the audience; frequency of the need is unknown.
  • Differentiated: hypothesis against paths emphasizing one platform; uniqueness unestablished.
  • Defensible: not yet passed; coverage is a requirement, not an implemented capability.
  • Evidence needed: corresponding scenarios verified against sources and independent transfer between contexts. Status: unverified-claim P2.

P3 — Reasoned recovery

  • Candidate claim: choose a recovery strategy and explain its effect on shared work.
  • Relevant: plausible need to validate.
  • Differentiated: alternatives already cover conflicts and recovery; the hypothesis concerns evaluating the reasoning.
  • Defensible: not yet passed; simulator and learning results are unavailable.
  • Evidence needed: rubrics evaluating choice, consequences, and preservation of others' work in a new incident. Status: unverified-claim P3.

None of these candidates is presented as a validated pillar. Confirming the messaging architecture does not mean passing the pillar test.

7. Anti-messages bank

Wording to avoid: IT: «Git per tutti», «soluzione completa», «impara senza fatica», «diventa esperto rapidamente». EN equivalents: “Git for everyone”, “complete solution”, “learn effortlessly”, “become an expert quickly”. Replace these with a specific action or outcome from the path, without generic promises or unverified time claims.

Frames not to imitate: visualization as the exclusive promise of LearnGitBranching, learning GitHub inside GitHub as the frame of GitHub Skills, and a game with real Git as the frame of Oh My Git!.

Unsupported claims: IT: «riduce gli errori», «garantisce autonomia», «più efficace», «accessibile a tutti». EN equivalents: “reduces errors”, “guarantees independence”, “more effective”, “accessible to everyone”. These respectively require observed results, transfer tests, appropriate comparisons, and accessibility checks. Do not use them as facts; they remain unverified-claim.

5. Demand plan

Consolidated from the confirmed demand plan, without new forecasts.

Starting point

No baseline for visits, starts, completions, or learning is available. Strictly zero service cost, an independent public course, three tiers, and complete Italian/English content at launch are confirmed. Social providers may be introduced in phases consistent with the budget constraint.

An example used in a private project review is not a partial launch. This plan does not execute publication, invitations, campaigns, or service changes.

Design principles

  1. A concrete problem leads to the course: a useful explanation and a link to the relevant exercise, without pages created only to capture searches.
  2. Observable evidence precedes a claim: examples and feedback explain what the course offers; independence and effectiveness remain hypotheses to test.
  3. Maintenance limits distribution: IT/EN content and corrections come first, followed by any additional channels.

Google recommends content useful to people. Organic presence does not require payment, but crawling, indexing, and ranking are not guaranteed. People-first content, Search Essentials.

Engine architecture

E1: Git problem → verified IT/EN explanation → curriculum → E2.

E2: worked example → understanding the method → tier choice → first exercise → E3.

E3: observed difficulty → reasoned IT/EN correction → possible voluntary return; recurring cases update E1 and E2.

E1 supports discovery; E2 evaluation and starting; E3 fit and return visits. Qualification means understanding whom the path serves and which problems it addresses, rather than evaluating sales leads. Engines are repeatable activities, not a channel list.

Engine detail

E1 — Searchable practical scenarios

Input: correct exercises, IT/EN explanations, sources, and links to the curriculum. Mechanism: a problem generates a useful page; observed questions guide revisions. Output: relevant visits to the path, if the page is discovered. Indicators: impressions, clicks, query relevance, curriculum visits where measurable, and requested corrections. Requirements: technical and linguistic review, accessible pages. First signal: an impression or click after discovery, without a guaranteed date. Search Console metrics define impressions/clicks/CTR; these data do not demonstrate learning.

E2 — Worked example → course

Input: a representative case spanning change, history, and review, with an explained outcome. Mechanism: the participant evaluates the method and finds a starting point. Output: an informed exercise start. Indicators: understanding of the task, verified starts, first completion, access barriers, and IT/EN differences. Requirements: verified example and a link to the path. First signal: a person tries the example; before launch, only during a private review.

E3 — Feedback → revision → return

Input: voluntary observations, reproducible errors, and test results. Mechanism: classify difficulties, correct material/translations, and explain the update; the participant chooses whether to return or share the course. Output: a corrected path and new voluntary opportunities to use it. Indicators: reproducible problems, resolutions, recurrence after correction, independent tests on new cases, and observable returns without extra tracking. Requirements: triage owner and revision capacity. First plannable signal: pilot feedback within the initial 90-day horizon, if people and material are available. This is not a promise to obtain users or results by a date.

The 90 days are a horizon, not a delivery date or traffic commitment.

Operating requirements

Responsibility for content, IT/EN review, and triage, possibly held by one person; no assignment is automatic. The budget does not estimate pre-launch development or translation.

Use only reliable aggregate data or documented pilot observations; no dashboard justifies paid services or collecting additional personal data. Search Console and feedback tools have not yet been set up.

8. Execution readiness

8.1 Conversion design: from discovery to learning

Pages/example → tier → first exercise → outcome → resume. Define objectives, rubrics, feedback, and tier transitions. Without this work, visits and starts do not demonstrate understanding. It requires instructional design, UX, and testing with participants, rather than a sales process or CRM.

8.2 Deployment: activation and maintenance

Build and verify the product; prepare IT/EN content, feedback, and procedures before activating channels. Required capabilities include TypeScript/Git, authentication, data, accessibility, translation, and maintenance. The 1–2 hours/week is the post-launch budget only. If the initial scope remains incomplete, postpone release; do not reduce languages/tiers or activate paid services.

Maintenance covers triage and small revisions, not a guarantee of every fix. Major incidents may require pausing distribution or the online service while planning a correction. No commercial SLA is promised.

8.3 Measurement: sources, review, and decisions

API for confirmed starts/completions; voluntary observations for rubrics and problems; Search Console signals only when configured and available. No invented traffic or new personal trackers merely to complete a dashboard. The maintainer role must periodically read signals and decide which corrections or channels are sustainable. Without this routine, an outcome cannot be attributed to an engine or the workload budget confirmed.

Capacity and operational start

The plan requires no paid supplier or hire. Human roles remain to be assigned, without assuming availability from the repository owner's name alone.

The reviewable operational scope specifies the outcome, candidate dependencies, initial leaderboard rules, and publication boundary. After approval, local implementation may start through subagents in separate worktrees, with checks and documentation updates. Account/secret setup, resources, deployment CI, pushes/PRs, and publication are specific later steps, not authorized by GTM-document confirmation alone.
